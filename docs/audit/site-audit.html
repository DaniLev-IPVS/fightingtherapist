The job, in four beats

Main problems

1

The legitimacy proof is buried High

What we saw
  • "Pro Fighter 1-0-0" only appears in the Instagram bio embed
  • Press (Global News, TriStar, Devon Larratt) only on /press
  • No credentials, certifications, years in practice or client count anywhere
Why it hurts

A curious visitor scrolls the homepage once. If they don't see proof in that scroll, they leave thinking "another trainer."

Fix direction

A proof bar under the hero (record, rating, press logos, years), then a "Why trust Zack" section with credentials and featured client stories.

2

The hero doesn't say who he is High

What we saw
  • Headline: "Where the battle for your best self begins"
  • Name and title only in a small caption under the video
  • Video is 12 seconds in a ~380px box
Why it hurts

The first five seconds decide everything. A slogan could belong to any gym.

Fix direction

Big, bold headline that states the claim (pro fighter who fixes athletes' injuries), full-bleed photo or video of him, rating and record in the same screen.

3

Services don't match what people actually buy High

What we saw
  • Site sells Athletic Therapy, Personal Training, MMA
  • Reviews keep praising nutrition, weight cutting and remote programs
  • Online Program link only in the footer, pointing at a French page
Why it hurts

Visitors can't find their problem, and Zack can't send them to one focused page.

Fix direction

Confirm the current offer list, then give each offer its own landing page. Homepage routes by problem: "I'm injured", "I fight", "I want to transform".

4

No lead tracking at all High

What we saw
  • Link-in-bio carries utm_source=ig, utm_medium=social; nothing stores it
  • Contact and booking forms post to WordPress / email
  • No pipeline, no status, no history per lead
Why it hurts

Zack can't tell which channel (IG, TikTok, ads) brings paying clients, so he can't double down on what works.

Fix direction

Capture UTMs and referrer on first visit, attach them to every form submission, and store leads in his own CRM with pipeline stages. That's step 5.

5

Confusing conversion path Medium

What we saw
  • "Get in touch" (free) and "Book a consultation" ($50, non-refundable) compete
  • No form on the homepage
  • No explanation of what happens after you submit
Why it hurts

Every extra decision drops people. A paywall before trust is built is a big ask.

Fix direction

One primary action per page, a short qualifying form (goal, injury, location, online or in person), and a "what happens next" strip with 3 steps.

6

Polish errors undercut trust Medium

What we saw
  • Benbouchta, Benboutcha, Benbouchta Martinovic, Zackaria; Zack, Zak, Zach
  • "Athlectic", "Therary", "Personl Training", "Consultatation"
  • 514-867-5954 in the footer, 514-558-6610 on Contact
Why it hurts

For a clinician, typos read as carelessness. Two phone numbers means some calls go nowhere.

Fix direction

Zakaria Benbouchta in full, Zack for short, everywhere. One phone number. Copy proofread in both languages.

7

Template design, generic copy Medium

What we saw
  • Content squeezed into a narrow centered column
  • Blog carousel shows the same 3 posts three times over
  • Paragraphs built from the same filler words across all pages
Why it hurts

Nothing feels like a fighter's brand. It doesn't match the energy of his videos.

Fix direction

Bold condensed type, full-width sections, big photos, real numbers. Rewrite copy in his own voice from the videos. (We keep the substance; structure and wording get tightened.)

8

Heavy, slow WordPress build Medium

What we saw
  • Divi 4 plus about 10 plugins (reviews, Instagram, booking, multilingual)
  • 4.1 s to load on desktop, 88 requests, 57 scripts
  • Hero and service sections duplicated in the HTML for mobile
Why it hurts

Visitors from social are on phones. Every second of load loses some of them, and Google ranks slow pages lower.

Fix direction

Rebuild on Next.js on Vercel (your GitHub repo + Vercel project), with forms, CRM and portals in the same codebase.
